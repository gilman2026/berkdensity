BEHIND THE MAP

Good maps show their working.

This is a map of residential population density in 2020, not today's population, pedestrian activity, building height, or the number of people with a particular street address.

The smallest reliable starting point

The polygons are 2020 Census tabulation blocks from the U.S. Census Bureau's TIGERweb service. Blocks often follow streets, but can also follow railways, shorelines, and other boundaries. They are not parcels or individual buildings.

Two views of density

People per acre = POP100 (resident population) ÷ (AREALAND in square meters ÷ 4,046.8564224). Homes per acre uses HU100 (housing units) with the same denominator. Housing units include vacant units; this is not an occupied-home count. Blocks without land have no density value, even if they have residents. One waterfront block reports 21 residents and 9 housing units but zero land area; those counts are retained, not discarded.

What a street profile means

We draw a 30-meter buffer around the selected city street centerlines and find Berkeley Census blocks intersecting it. We count each matching block once, using its entire population and land area. The profile density is total residents (or housing units) divided by total land acres across those blocks, not an average of individual densities. Blocks can belong to several street profiles, including blocks near intersections. A street profile covers the full named street in this dataset, not just the part currently on screen.

Do not read the street totals as people living on that street or within 30 meters of it. No people are assigned to individual addresses or modeled along a street.

A city average is not a typical block

The city benchmark includes all Berkeley land, including parks, campus, industrial areas, and the waterfront. It is not net residential density. Very small blocks can have unusually high densities. Population includes group quarters such as student dormitories, which are not counted as ordinary housing units; a high residents-to-housing ratio is a clue, not proof of a dormitory.

Time, privacy, and uncertainty

Census counts reflect April 1, 2020, and are subject to Census disclosure-avoidance protections, including statistical noise at small geographic levels. New housing and demographic changes since 2020 are not shown. The street geometry is a later city GIS snapshot, so it may not align perfectly with 2020 blocks.

Basemap & satellite imagery

Map uses Esri's Light Gray Canvas basemap and reference labels. Satellite uses Esri World Imagery, which combines satellite and aerial photography. Imagery dates vary and do not necessarily match the 2020 Census. Density colors are more transparent in satellite mode; uncheck Density for an unobstructed view. Census block outlines and background city centerlines are not drawn over the basemap; only your selection is highlighted.

Display geometry is rounded to six decimal places; street lines are generalized by roughly one meter. Calculations use Census-reported land area, not the displayed polygon area. Mapping: Leaflet; spatial operations: Turf. No analytics, tracking code, location requests, or paid API keys. CDN libraries and Esri tiles require an internet connection and send normal browser requests to those providers. Street searches and density calculations use the bundled data locally.
